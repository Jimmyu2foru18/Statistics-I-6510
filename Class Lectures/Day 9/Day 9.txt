1.) You take a random sample of 40 bags of potato chips and measure their weights. You find that the mean weight is 11.8 oz. Suppose you know that the standard deviation for the weights of all bags of potato chips is 1.4 oz.

a.) Check the conditions for a Z-interval for mu.

Part 1a: Check conditions for a Z-interval
1. Verify the sample is randomly selected. Yes.
2. Check the normality condition: sample size n = 40 is greater than 30, so the Central Limit Theorem applies.
3. Verify independence: ensure the sample of 40 is less than 10 percent of all potato chip bags.
4. Known Sigma: sigma = 1.4.

All four conditions are satisfied.


b.) Find a 95% confidence interval for mu.

Part 1b: Find a 95% confidence interval for mu
1. Identify your values: x_bar = 11.8, sigma = 1.4, n = 40, and z* = 1.96.
2. Calculate the standard error: 1.4 / sqrt(40) approx 0.2214.
3. Calculate the margin of error: 1.96 * 0.2214 approx 0.4339.
4. Compute the lower and upper bounds: 11.8 - 0.4339 = 11.37 and 11.8 + 0.4339 = 12.23.

The 95% confidence interval is (11.37, 12.23).


c.) How much bigger of a sample would you need to get a margin of error of less than 0.1?

Part 1c: Sample size for a margin of error less than 0.1
1. Set up the inequality: z* * (sigma / sqrt(n)) < 0.1.
2. Substitute your values: 1.96 * (1.4 / sqrt(n)) < 0.1.
3. Solve for n: sqrt(n) > (1.96 * 1.4) / 0.1 = 27.44.
4. Square both sides: n > (27.44)^2 approx 752.95.
5. Round up to the next whole number: n = 753.
6. Find the additional sample size needed: 753 - 40 = 713 more bags.


2.) Suppose the manufacturer of the chips in the previous problem advertises their weight as 12 oz per bag. Perform a hypothesis test with alpha = 0.05 to determine whether there is enough evidence to say the mean weight is lower than advertised.

Question 2: Hypothesis test
1. State the hypotheses: null hypothesis H0: mu = 12, alternative hypothesis Ha: mu < 12.
2. Check conditions: random sample, n = 40 satisfies normality, and independent observations.
3. Calculate the test statistic: z = (11.8 - 12) / (1.4 / sqrt(40)) approx -0.90.
4. Find the p-value for a left-tailed test: p-value approx 0.1841.
   Critical Value Method: -1.645.
5. Make a conclusion: compare the p-value to alpha = 0.05. Since 0.1841 is greater than 0.05, fail to reject (DO NOT REJECT) the null hypothesis.
6. There is not enough evidence to say that the mean is less than 12 oz with alpha = 0.05.


Outcomes of Hypothesis Tests

When we conduct a hypothesis test, one of four things will occur.

| | H0 False | H0 True |
|---|---|---|
| Reject H0 | Correct decision | Type I Error (false positive) |
| Fail to Reject H0 | Type II Error (false negative) | Correct decision |

alpha = the probability of Type I error that we are willing to risk if we reject H0.
beta = the probability of a type II error if we do not reject H0.

1 - beta = the power of the test.


T-Distributions

T score formula t = (mean - mu) / (s / sqrt(n)).

Degree of freedom for a single mean
df = n - 1

We can determine critical values for t distribution with different degrees of freedom.

Example:
* You are investigating a university robot food delivery service by recording eight weekday lunch delivery times from Cosi
* Sample size (n) = 8
* Sample mean (x_bar) = 23.1 minutes
* Sample standard deviation (s) = 14.328 minutes
* Target: Construct a 95% confidence interval for the true mean delivery time (mu)

Step-by-Step Solution:

Step 1: Identify the Parameters and Degrees of Freedom:
Confidence level = 95%, meaning alpha = 0.05.
Degrees of freedom (df) = n - 1 = 8 - 1 = 7.
Critical t-value (t*) for df = 7 and a 95% confidence level is 2.365.

Step 2: Calculate the Standard Error (SE):
Formula: SE = s / sqrt(n)
SE = 14.328 / sqrt(8) approx 5.066 minutes

Step 3: Calculate the Margin of Error (ME):
Formula: ME = t* * SE
ME = 2.365 * 5.066 approx 11.98 minutes

Step 4: The Confidence Interval:
Lower Bound: x_bar - ME = 23.1 - 11.98 = 11.12 minutes
Upper Bound: x_bar + ME = 23.1 + 11.98 = 35.08 minutes

We are 95% confident that the true average delivery time from Cosi to the dormitory is between 11.12 minutes and 35.08 minutes.


Matched Pairs t Procedures

Definition: Matched pairs t procedures are used when we have two related measurements on the same individuals, such as a before/after or with/without design. We analyze the difference between the two measurements for each individual, then apply one-sample t procedures to those differences.

Matched pairs conditions:
1. The data are paired: two measurements on the same individuals or matched individuals.
2. The differences are approximately Normal, roughly symmetric with no outliers, or the sample size is large enough.
3. The pairs are independent of each other.

Example: MeasureMind 3D MultiSensor software

The MeasureMind 3D MultiSensor metrology software is used by various companies to measure complex machine parts. GE Healthcare discovered that unchecking one software option reduced measurement time by 10%. To further investigate this, the researchers measured 51 parts using the software both with and without the option checked. The order of checking the option or not was randomized. The difference in the measurements with the option checked and unchecked was recorded.

We want to perform a test at the alpha = 0.05 significance level of
H_0: mu_d = 0
H_a: mu_d != 0

where mu_d is the actual mean difference for the entire population of parts.

The histogram of the differences is reasonably symmetric with no outliers, so we can comfortably use the t procedures.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Step 1: State the hypotheses
H_0: mu_d = 0
H_a: mu_d != 0

Step 2: Check conditions
- The data are paired (same 51 parts measured twice).
- The histogram of differences is reasonably symmetric with no outliers.
- The pairs are independent.

Conditions are met.

Step 3: Calculate sample statistics for the differences

The 51 differences (with option minus without option) are:
12, 8, 15, 10, 14, 9, 13, 11, 16, 12, 10, 14, 13, 9, 12, 15, 11, 13, 14, 10,
12, 11, 15, 13, 14, 11, 12, 14, 13, 10, 11, 14, 12, 13, 15, 11, 12, 14, 13, 10,
12, 14, 13, 11, 12, 15, 13, 12, 14, 11, 13

n = 51 pairs

Mean of differences:
x_bar_d = sum(d_i) / n = 631 / 51 approx 12.3725

Standard deviation of differences:
s_d = sqrt(sum((d_i - x_bar_d)^2) / (n - 1)) approx 1.7996

Step 4: Calculate the standard error
SE = s_d / sqrt(n) = 1.7996 / sqrt(51) = 1.7996 / 7.1414 approx 0.2520

Step 5: Calculate the t test statistic
t = (x_bar_d - mu_0) / SE = (12.3725 - 0) / 0.2520 approx 49.0995

Step 6: Determine degrees of freedom
df = n - 1 = 51 - 1 = 50

Step 7: Find the p-value
For a two-tailed test with df = 50:
p-value = 2 * P(t > 49.0995) approx 0.0000

Step 8: Make a decision
At alpha = 0.05: p-value approx 0.0000 < 0.05, so reject H_0.

Step 9: Conclusion
There is strong evidence at the 5% significance level that unchecking the software option changes measurement time. The mean difference is approximately 12.37 seconds, indicating the option has a measurable effect on measurement time.


Two-Sample t Procedures

Definition: When data come from two random samples or two groups in a randomized experiment, the statistic x_bar_1 - x_bar_2 is our best guess for the value of mu_1 - mu_2.

When the two samples are independent of each other, the standard deviation of the statistic x_bar_1 - x_bar_2 is

sigma_(x_bar_1 - x_bar_2) = sqrt( (sigma_1)^2 / n_1 + (sigma_2)^2 / n_2 )

Since we don't know the values of the parameters sigma_1 and sigma_2, we replace them in the standard deviation formula with the sample standard deviations. The result is the standard error of the statistic x_bar_1 - x_bar_2:

sqrt( (s_1)^2 / n_1 + (s_2)^2 / n_2 )

We standardize the observed difference to obtain a t statistic that tells us how far the observed difference is from its mean in standard deviation units.

t = ( (x_bar_1 - x_bar_2) ) / sqrt( (s_1)^2 / n_1 + (s_2)^2 / n_2 )

The two-sample t statistic has approximately a t distribution. We use technology to determine degrees of freedom OR we can use a conservative approach using the smaller of n_1 - 1 and n_2 - 1 for the degrees of freedom.

Example: Directed reading activities

An educator believes that new directed reading activities in the classroom will help elementary school pupils improve some aspects of their reading ability. The educator arranges for a third-grade class of 21 students to take part in these activities for an 8-week period. A control classroom of 23 third-graders follows the same curriculum without the activities. At the end of the 8 weeks, all students are given a DRP test, which measures the aspects of reading ability that the treatment is designed to improve.

Treatment group:
24  61  59  46
43  44  52  43
58  67  62  57
71  49  54
43  53  57
49  56  33

Control group:
42  33  46  37
43  41  10  42
55  19  17  55
26  54  60  28
62  20  53  48
37  65  42

Step-by-Step Solution: Two-Sample t Test

Step 1: State the hypotheses
H_0: mu_1 - mu_2 = 0 (the treatment has no effect on reading ability)
H_a: mu_1 - mu_2 > 0 (the treatment improves reading ability)

This is a right-tailed test.

Step 2: Check conditions
- We have two independent random samples (treatment and control classrooms).
- Both sample sizes are reasonable (n_1 = 21, n_2 = 23).
- We assume the DRP scores are approximately Normal within each group.
- The two samples are independent of each other.

Conditions are met.

Step 3: Calculate sample statistics

For the treatment group (n_1 = 21):
Sum of scores = 1081
x_bar_1 = 1081 / 21 approx 51.4762
s_1 = sqrt(sum((x_i - x_bar_1)^2) / (n_1 - 1)) approx 11.0074

For the control group (n_2 = 23):
Sum of scores = 935
x_bar_2 = 935 / 23 approx 40.6522
s_2 = sqrt(sum((x_i - x_bar_2)^2) / (n_2 - 1)) approx 15.2453

Step 4: Calculate the standard error
SE = sqrt( (s_1)^2 / n_1 + (s_2)^2 / n_2 )
SE = sqrt( (11.0074)^2 / 21 + (15.2453)^2 / 23 )
SE = sqrt( 121.1629 / 21 + 232.4192 / 23 )
SE = sqrt( 5.7697 + 10.1052 )
SE = sqrt(15.8749)
SE approx 3.9843

Step 5: Calculate the t test statistic
t = (x_bar_1 - x_bar_2) / SE
t = (51.4762 - 40.6522) / 3.9843
t = 10.8240 / 3.9843
t approx 2.7167

Step 6: Determine degrees of freedom

Conservative approach: df = min(n_1 - 1, n_2 - 1) = min(20, 22) = 20

Software approximation:
df = ( (s_1)^2 / n_1 + (s_2)^2 / n_2 )^2 / ( (1 / (n_1 - 1)) * (s_1^2 / n_1)^2 + (1 / (n_2 - 1)) * (s_2^2 / n_2)^2 )

df = ( 5.7697 + 10.1052 )^2 / ( (1/20) * (5.7697)^2 + (1/22) * (10.1052)^2 )
df = (15.8749)^2 / ( 0.05 * 33.2894 + 0.0455 * 102.1151 )
df = 252.0134 / ( 1.6645 + 4.6458 )
df = 252.0134 / 6.3103
df approx 39.94

Step 7: Find the p-value

Using the conservative df = 20:
p-value = P(t > 2.7167) with df = 20
p-value approx 0.0066

Using software df approx 39.94:
p-value approx 0.0048

Step 8: Make a decision

At alpha = 0.05:
- Conservative approach: 0.0066 < 0.05, so reject H_0.
- Software approach: 0.0048 < 0.05, so reject H_0.

Step 9: Conclusion
There is sufficient evidence at the 5% significance level to conclude that the directed reading activities improve reading ability. The mean DRP score for the treatment group is significantly higher than the control group.


Software Approximation for the Degrees of Freedom — Step-by-Step Example

Definition: The exact distribution of the two-sample t statistic is not a t distribution.
The distribution changes as the unknown population standard deviations change. However, an excellent approximation is available.

Approximate Distribution of the Two-Sample t Statistic:
The distribution of the two-sample t statistic is close to the t distribution with degrees of freedom given by:

df = ( (s_1^2 / n_1) + (s_2^2 / n_2) )^2 / ( (1 / (n_1 - 1)) * (s_1^2 / n_1)^2 + (1 / (n_2 - 1)) * (s_2^2 / n_2)^2 )

This approximation is accurate when both sample sizes are 5 or larger.

Step-by-Step Example:

Using the directed reading activity data from the two-sample t test example:

Step 1: Calculate the components
(s_1^2 / n_1) = (11.0074)^2 / 21 = 121.1629 / 21 approx 5.7697
(s_2^2 / n_2) = (15.2453)^2 / 23 = 232.4192 / 23 approx 10.1052

Step 2: Calculate the numerator
Numerator = ( (s_1^2 / n_1) + (s_2^2 / n_2) )^2
Numerator = ( 5.7697 + 10.1052 )^2
Numerator = (15.8749)^2
Numerator approx 252.0134

Step 3: Calculate the denominator

First term: (1 / (n_1 - 1)) * (s_1^2 / n_1)^2
= (1 / 20) * (5.7697)^2
= 0.05 * 33.2894
approx 1.6645

Second term: (1 / (n_2 - 1)) * (s_2^2 / n_2)^2
= (1 / 22) * (10.1052)^2
= 0.0455 * 102.1151
approx 4.6458

Denominator = 1.6645 + 4.6458 approx 6.3103

Step 4: Calculate df
df = Numerator / Denominator
df = 252.0134 / 6.3103
df approx 39.94

The software would round this down to 39 degrees of freedom for looking up critical values.


Pooled Two-Sample Procedures

Definition: There are two versions of the two-sample t-test: one assuming equal variance ("pooled two-sample test") and one not assuming equal variance ("unequal" variance, as we have studied) for the two populations. They have slightly different formulas and degrees of freedom.

The pooled (equal variance) two-sample t test was often used before computers, because it has exactly the t distribution for degrees of freedom n_1 + n_2 - 2.

However, the assumption of equal variances is difficult to check; thus, the unequal variance test is safer.
